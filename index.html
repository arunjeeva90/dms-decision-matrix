Purpose of this interactive decision reference

What this page defines

This webpage is an interactive engineering reference for the Driver Monitoring System decision logic. It is not a marketing feature page.

The purpose is to convert raw driver observations such as eye closure, gaze direction, head pose, phone use and camera confidence into clear system-level outputs such as Normal, Monitor, Warning, Danger, Critical and DMS Degraded.

It helps align software, validation, functional safety, ADAS fusion and partner teams before implementation. Instead of discussing DMS features generally, it defines decision rules, thresholds, warning levels, false-alert suppression logic and ADAS interface expectations.

Why this is useful

  • Interactive decision review: documents how driver observations are converted into DMS states, alert levels and ADAS-ready outputs.
  • Not a generic feature list: defines how driver states are classified, escalated, suppressed or degraded based on evidence and duration.
  • Cross-functional alignment: supports ADAS, DMS, validation, functional safety, software and partner discussions using one version-controlled reference.
  • Reviewable engineering artifact: searchable, interactive, version-controlled and easier to update than a static slide deck.
  • Decision freeze support: helps freeze logic before coding, validation planning and CAN interface definition.
  • Business value: faster partner alignment, clearer requirements, fewer rework cycles and stronger system-definition maturity.

Threshold guidance

Prototype defaults only. Final timing must be calibrated using Indian cabin-road data and homologation evidence.

Off-road gaze

Monitor around 1.5–2s, Warning beyond 2s, Danger when sustained beyond 3–4s.

Downward / phone-lap gaze

Monitor around 1–1.5s, Warning beyond 2s, Danger beyond 3s especially with phone or hand cue.

Eye closure

Normal blink below 300–400ms. Warning above 800ms. Danger/microsleep beyond 1500ms.

PERCLOS

Short-window trend: observe below 0.15, warn above 0.25, danger above 0.40.

No face / no eyes

Short transients below 0.5s are tolerated. More than 1s reduces confidence. Sustained loss becomes DMS degraded/unavailable.

No response after warning

If the driver does not recover after warning for 2–3s, escalate availability risk and trigger urgent HMI/ADAS fallback.

Important: Do not use timing values as homologation constants. They are engineering defaults for prototyping and validation planning.
6

Cumulative-state and recovery logic

Single-event logic

One long off-road gaze → Warning
One long eye closure → Danger
One phone-in-hand + gaze-down → Danger

Cumulative logic

Many short off-road glances within 30 s → Warning
Repeated slow eyelid closures within 60 s → Drowsiness Warning
Repeated head nods within 30 s → Drowsiness Danger

Recovery logic

Do not remove warning immediately when one frame becomes normal. This prevents flicker.

Warning ON = condition true for threshold duration
Warning OFF = road gaze stable for 1.0–2.0 s
Danger OFF = driver recovers + gaze stable + eyes open + no phone cue

Processing flow: confidence, sanctioned task, fusion

Valid mirror, cluster, junction and parking glances are handled as a pre-filter before distraction classification. This avoids false alerts without suppressing true drowsiness or microsleep evidence.

STEP 1DMS confidence check

Camera quality, face visibility, eye confidence and degraded reason.

STEP 2Sanctioned-task pre-filter

Mirror, cluster, junction, reverse or parking glance allowed only within limits.

STEP 3Independent heads

Drowsiness, distraction, availability and confidence stay separate.

STEP 4Risk fusion

Fuse driver-state risk with forward ADAS risk such as TTC or LDW.

STEP 5Alert arbitration

Apply hysteresis, reason code and HMI/CAN output.

IF gaze_away_is_sanctioned AND duration_within_limit → Normal / Monitor
ELSE IF sustained_offroad_or_phone_or_drowsiness → Warning / Danger
IF dms_confidence_low → DMS Degraded / Unavailable

ADAS readiness output contract

The DMS output is useful only when it becomes an ADAS-ready interface. The forward ADAS stack should know whether the driver is attentive, distracted, drowsy, unavailable or whether the DMS itself is degraded.

SignalPurposeExample values
DMS_Confidence_StateTrust level of DMS observationHIGH, MEDIUM, LOW, UNAVAILABLE
DMS_Driver_AvailabilityWhether driver can respond to ADAS warningAVAILABLE, PARTIAL, UNAVAILABLE, UNKNOWN
DMS_Drowsiness_LevelDrowsiness/microsleep outputNORMAL, EARLY, DROWSY, MICROSLEEP
DMS_Distraction_LevelVisual/manual/cognitive distractionNONE, MONITOR, WARNING, DANGER
DMS_Primary_Reason_CodeWhy the output was triggeredPHONE_GAZE_DOWN, MICROSLEEP, NO_RESPONSE
DMS_Degraded_Reason_CodeWhy DMS confidence is reducedCAMERA_BLOCKED, EYES_OCCLUDED, GLARE

Driver-aware ADAS example: If forward TTC risk is high and DMS says driver is not looking at the road, the warning can be escalated earlier than a normal FCW threshold.
