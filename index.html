New Risk Level: Monitor

A new intermediate state has been added between Normal and Warning to reduce false positives and acknowledge states that aren't yet unsafe but require observation.

Normal
Fully acceptable driving behavior
e.g. Eyes open, road gaze
→
Monitor
Not yet unsafe, but watch closely
e.g. Short infotainment glance
→
Warning
Sustained distraction/drowsiness risk
e.g. Looking down > threshold
→
Danger
Immediate safety risk
e.g. Microsleep, texting + gaze down
→
Critical
Driver unavailable or DMS unreliable
e.g. No face, camera blocked
Key Insight: Not every "head down" or "head turned" state should immediately become Warning. Many cases should first transition to Monitor to reduce alert fatigue.

5

Cumulative-State Logic

A. Single-Event Logic

One long off-road gaze → Warning One long eye closure → Danger One phone-in-hand + gaze-down → Danger

B. Cumulative Logic

Many short off-road glances within 30 s → Warning Repeated slow eyelid closures within 60 s → Drowsiness Warning Repeated head nods within 30 s → Drowsiness Danger

C. Recovery Logic

Do not remove the warning immediately when one frame becomes normal. This prevents flickering.

Warning ON = condition true for threshold duration Warning OFF = road gaze stable for 1.0–2.0 s Danger OFF = driver recovers + gaze stable + eyes open + no phone cue
Anti-flicker: Prevents unstable transitions like
NORMAL → DEGRADED → WARNING → NORMAL → WARNING

Final Output Architecture

The matrix outputs four independent classification heads that are fused into a single driver risk score.

😴
Drowsiness State
PERCLOS, blink, head nod
📵
Distraction State
Gaze, phone, visual-manual
🚨
Availability State
Responsiveness, posture
📷
DMS Confidence
Camera, face detection, quality

Final Decision Fusion

Final Driver Risk = max(
    Drowsiness Risk,
    Distraction Risk,
    Driver Unavailability Risk,
    ADAS-Fusion Risk,
    DMS-Degraded Risk
)

This makes the system OEM-ready and much more stable than a simple table-based classifier.
